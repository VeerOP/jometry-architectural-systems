Section 02 · Helmholtz Geometry

2.0The cavity is the absorber

0.95Peak NRC absorption
50–220mm adjustable cavity
125–4kHz tuned frequency range
0 toolsToolless tile exchange

Most acoustic panels are a material problem. Ours is a geometry problem.

A slotted face over an air cavity behaves as a resonant Helmholtz absorber. The depth of that cavity determines which frequencies it absorbs — deeper cavity, lower bass frequencies.

Our mounting standoff is adjustable. The same tile, set at a different depth, targets a different part of the spectrum. The wall is tuned specifically to the room rather than forcing the room to accept standard generic foam.

Long-Term Reconfigurability

Because every tile is demountable, the wall can be re-tuned, repaired, or reconfigured long after handover without wet trades or repainting.

Section 03 · Acoustic Mechanics

3.0How the system absorbs

Sound enters through the slots and perforations in the tile face. Within the cavity, the air mass and the absorptive infill convert acoustic energy to heat.

The standoff depth sets the absorption peak. The open area of the face and the infill density set how much is absorbed and how broadly across octaves.

Three variables, one tile

Standoff depth, face open area and cavity infill are specified per zone — so a single tile family serves a control room, a boardroom and a corridor without a different product for each.

Performance Validation

Panels are tested to ASTM C423 and ISO 354 reverberation standards. Certified absorption coefficients are provided with every engineering specification.
