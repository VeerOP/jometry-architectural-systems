2.0 The same job, two ways

Adding a data outlet or repairing an internal line in an existing wall elevation.

Conventional Drywall Partition

  1. Isolate area & erect plastic dust containment tent
  2. Cut open board with power drywall saw
  3. Pull new cabling / chase electrical fault
  4. Patch backing board, tape joints, skim wet plaster
  5. Wait 24–48 hours curing and drying time
  6. Sand smooth — generating fine airborne silica dust
  7. Repaint entire wall elevation for color match
  8. Clean down and reinstate occupied space

  1. Toolless unclip of targeted 500×500 mm cassette
  2. Route new cabling through segregated raceway
  3. Clip cassette firmly back onto concealed node

4.0 Anatomy of the cavity

Panels stand clear of the substrate on adjustable standoffs, creating a continuous, ventilated raceway behind the finished elevation.

This cavity organizes power, high-speed data, HVAC distribution, and acoustic absorption into isolated rails rather than burying them haphazardly inside studs.

Zone-Configured Cavity Depth

Standoff depth is specified per architectural zone: 50 mm for power/data lines, 100–180 mm for deep acoustic Helmholtz resonators, ventilation ducts, and irrigation manifolds.

Universal Sub-frame Grid

The identical aluminum rail mounts acoustic fluted tiles, living bio-cassettes, and illuminated panels interchangeably.

Section 06 · Helmholtz Cavity Tuning

6.0 The cavity is the absorber

Most acoustic panels are a material problem. Ours is a geometry problem.

A slotted face over an air cavity acts as a Helmholtz resonant absorber. The depth of that cavity determines which frequencies are absorbed — deeper cavity, lower bass resonance.

With D'WALL's adjustable standoff, the same tile targets different parts of the spectrum simply by adjusting cavity depth. The wall is tuned specifically to the room rather than forcing the room to accept generic acoustic foam.
